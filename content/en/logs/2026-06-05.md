Today I practiced English expressions related to mathematics, statistics, and machine learning. These are areas where I struggle to verbalize things naturally in English, even when I understand the concepts themselves.

Here are some of the phrases I worked on:

- *The probability will be 26 over 81.* — Fractions are read as "numerator over denominator."
- *The fourth root of 16 is 2.* — "The nth root of X" is the standard way to express radicals.
- *The answer is three point two multiplied by ten to the power of 6.* — Scientific notation uses "times" or "multiplied by" between the coefficient and the power of ten. Note that "multiplies" should be "multiplied by" here.
- *We take the derivative of f of x with respect to x.* — Calculus expressions follow a fixed pattern: "derivative of [function] with respect to [variable]."
- *Logistic regression is useful to use numerical input variables and categorical output variables.* — A small correction here: "usefult" should be "useful," and the sentence reads more naturally as "Logistic regression is useful when you have numerical input variables and categorical output variables."

Mathematical and statistical vocabulary is tough to get used to. Writing out a formula on paper would convey the meaning instantly, but being able to say it aloud in English is a different skill altogether. I want to keep building from the basics.
