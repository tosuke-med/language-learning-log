## Today's practice phrases
What's your cut?
I totally messed up.
Are you messing with me?
We're screwed.
He pissed off and got out of the room.

## Correction and explanation from LLM

**"He pissed off and get out of the room."**
→ *He **pissed off and got** out of the room.*

"Get" should be "got" to match the past tense of "pissed off." Both verbs describe the same completed action, so they need to agree in tense.

The other four phrases are natural and correct as written.

## Daily English Expressions Built from Simple Words

Simple English words such as **cut**, **mess**, **piss**, and **screw** often become difficult when they are used in casual expressions or combined with prepositions.  
This list summarizes common informal expressions, their meanings, more polite alternatives, and example sentences.

## Three-level learning pattern

It is useful to learn these expressions in three levels: **casual**, **neutral**, and **formal**.

### Example 1

| Level | Expression |
|---|---|
| Casual | I screwed up. |
| Neutral | I made a mistake. |
| Formal | I made an error in judgment. |

- Casual: I screwed up the presentation.
- Neutral: I made a mistake during the presentation.
- Formal: I made an error in judgment during the presentation.

### Example 2

| Level | Expression |
|---|---|
| Casual | That doesn't cut it. |
| Neutral | That is not enough. |
| Formal | That does not meet the required standard. |

- Casual: This explanation doesn't cut it.
- Neutral: This explanation is not enough.
- Formal: This explanation does not meet the required standard.

### Example 3

| Level | Expression |
|---|---|
| Casual | Are you messing with me? |
| Neutral | Are you joking? |
| Formal | Are you being serious? |

- Casual: Are you messing with me?
- Neutral: Are you joking?
- Formal: Are you being serious?

### Example 4

| Level | Expression |
|---|---|
| Casual | He was pissed off. |
| Neutral | He was upset. |
| Formal | He was very dissatisfied with the situation. |

- Casual: He was pissed off about the decision.
- Neutral: He was upset about the decision.
- Formal: He was very dissatisfied with the decision.
