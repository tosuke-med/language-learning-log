Today I focused on slang expressions built around the word *freak*. I picked these up while watching Minecraft commentary videos, where this kind of casual language comes up constantly.

Here are the phrases I practiced, along with corrections and notes:

- *No freaking way.* — A strong emphatic denial. "Freaking" softens the intensity compared to a cruder intensifier, making it widely usable in casual speech.
- *I was totally freaked out when I saw the screen.* — The original "freak out" needs to be in the past tense: **freaked out**. "Freak out" is a phrasal verb meaning to become suddenly shocked or scared, so the past form is "freaked out."
- *She is a neat freak.* — A small but important fix: the article **a** is required before "neat freak." A *neat freak* is someone obsessively tidy — a fixed compound noun that always needs an article.
- *He is a freak of nature. He works three times as much as an ordinary person.* — "Freak of nature" is correct. The second sentence is more natural with **three times as much as** rather than "three times more than," which can sound ambiguous in English.
- *You're freaking me out.* — Perfectly natural. This is the causative use of "freak out": you are causing someone else to feel unsettled or scared.
