Today, I realized that creating presentations in the biological field requires using verbs and connectors appropriately to explain causal relationships in biological mechanisms.

I sometimes struggle to use a variety of expressions such as "induce," "drive," "trigger," "lead to," "result in," or "be linked to." However, these verbs are very important because they can clearly describe relationships between biological processes, molecules, or cellular events.

For example:

- Tau accumulation drives neurodegeneration.
- The mutation triggers abnormal protein aggregation.
- Reduced flexibility leads to impaired ligand binding.
- Mechanical stress induces conformational changes.

Causal explanation is essential in biological presentations because it helps the audience understand not only what happens, but also how and why it happens.

To create and deliver better academic presentations, I have been learning how to explain ideas more clearly in English. This website has been incredibly helpful for that purpose:

[https://www.assertion-evidence.org/](https://www.assertion-evidence.org/)

I also bought the book written by the author of the website, and it is very useful and convincing for researchers who want to improve their presentation skills.

However, the website mainly targets researchers in mechanics and material science. The biological field is somewhat different when it comes to explaining concepts and mechanisms — biology presentations often require careful explanations of causal relationships, molecular interactions, and dynamic biological processes. Because of this difference, I feel that I need to find more examples of high-quality presentations in the biological field, especially from academic conferences in English. I want to learn how experienced researchers explain complex biological mechanisms in a clear, logical, and natural way.
